### [SQLite&\#x27;s sqlite3\_rsync Can Snapshot Databases Over SSH Without a Local Copy](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/26/sqlite3-rsync/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A November 26, 2026 note from Simon Willison highlights a practical property of SQLite&\#x27;s sqlite3\_rsync: it can copy a database snapshot to another machine over SSH without first creating a full local duplicate. The tool was added as experimental in SQLite 3.47.0 in October 2024 and enhanced in 3.50.0 in May 2025; Willison notes he had written about this use when it first shipped. It can also update an existing replica by exchanging cryptographic hashes for pages or page groups and sending only differing content, with total bandwidth possibly a few percent above the database size when the two sides start vastly different.

rss · Simon Willison · Nov 26, 06:08

**「Background」** SQLite&\#x27;s backup API and VACUUM INTO produce a complete second copy, so a machine needs enough free disk for a duplicate before it can snapshot a database. sqlite3\_rsync avoids that local-copy step by using an rsync-like protocol over SSH, and it has remained labeled experimental since its introduction in SQLite 3.47.0.

**「Impact」** For operators on small instances where the database already occupies more than half the available disk, sqlite3\_rsync provides an off-machine snapshot path that the backup API and VACUUM INTO cannot provide locally; the tradeoff is reliance on an experimental tool and SSH connectivity to the replica.

### [Gene-edited pig kidney keeps working for one year in U.S. patient](https://www.solidot.org/story?sid=86003) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A gene-edited pig kidney has continued to function for one year in 58-year-old Molly Falco, who received the transplant at Massachusetts General Hospital on Nov. 22, 2025, Solidot reports. The kidney has produced urine, cleared toxins, and helped regulate fluid balance, and Falco has resumed driving, can cook for her family, and plans to ski again. The organ came from eGenesis pigs with 69 edits: three to remove pig antigens, seven to introduce human genes, and 59 to inactivate porcine endogenous retroviruses. It is a single-patient result, not evidence that pig kidneys work reliably in other recipients, and it is set against about 90,000 Americans awaiting a kidney and fewer than 26,000 transplants performed in 2025.

rss · Solidot · Nov 26, 14:49

**「Background」** Xenotransplantation uses organs from another species, and pigs are considered a viable donor source because their organs are similar in size and physiology to human organs. The main barrier is immune rejection: the human immune system recognizes specific molecules on pig cells and attacks the graft, while gene editing seeks to remove pig antigens that provoke rejection and introduce human genes that improve compatibility.

**「Impact」** For patients on kidney waiting lists, the immediate effect is limited: the report covers one recipient and provides no data on rejection episodes, immunosuppression, or function beyond 12 months. The 69-edit design, including 59 edits to inactivate porcine endogenous retroviruses, also indicates the rejection and cross-species infection safeguards that would need to hold in any broader use.

**「Background」** Disputes over whether a story, manuscript, or tweet was written by AI now arise constantly, often around tools such as Pangram. **「Solution」** Goedecke frames the question as one of gatekeeping power—over literary prizes, students, and public discourse—and steelmans the skeptics rather than dismissing them. He therefore argues that people who want to keep AI out of human art and culture should use the most powerful detection tools available, and that those who distrust tech companies should push for open-source detectors or government-built platforms instead. Otherwise, he says, more AI-written stories, articles, and poetry will enter those spaces.

**「Takeaway」** Goedecke’s larger point is that AI detection is a contest over who gets to enforce cultural and institutional boundaries, and that leaving those judgments to communities may prove less reliable than imperfect tool-based gatekeeping.
